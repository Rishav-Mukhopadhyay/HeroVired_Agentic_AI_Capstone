# Asthma in adults
Source: Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA) strategy report; educational summary.

## Treatment
GINA recommends that adults and adolescents receive inhaled corticosteroid (ICS)-containing treatment and not short-acting beta-agonist (SABA)-only treatment. Preferred reliever: as-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol; for more severe asthma, daily ICS-formoterol maintenance and reliever therapy. Check inhaler technique and adherence before stepping up.

## Exacerbations
Short course of oral corticosteroids (for example prednisolone 40-50 mg daily for 5-7 days) for moderate exacerbations.

## Red flags
Difficulty speaking, use of accessory muscles, oxygen saturation below 92%, silent chest or drowsiness require emergency care. Beta-blockers (especially non-selective) and NSAIDs in aspirin-sensitive patients can worsen asthma.
