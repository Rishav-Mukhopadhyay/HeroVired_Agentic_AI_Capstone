# Gout
Source: 2020 American College of Rheumatology Guideline for the Management of Gout; educational summary.

## Acute flare
Treat early with colchicine (low dose: 1.2 mg then 0.6 mg one hour later), an NSAID, or a glucocorticoid. Choose based on kidney function, interacting drugs and comorbidities. Do not stop urate-lowering therapy during a flare.

## Urate-lowering therapy
Indicated for frequent flares, tophi or radiographic damage. Allopurinol is first-line: start at 100 mg daily or less (lower in chronic kidney disease stage 3 or higher) and titrate to a serum urate below 6 mg/dL. Give flare prophylaxis (for example low-dose colchicine) for 3 to 6 months when starting. Consider HLA-B*58:01 testing before allopurinol in patients of Southeast Asian or African American descent.

## Safety
Colchicine interacts with strong CYP3A4 or P-glycoprotein inhibitors (for example clarithromycin) and needs dose reduction in kidney impairment. NSAIDs should be avoided in significant kidney disease, heart failure or with anticoagulants.
