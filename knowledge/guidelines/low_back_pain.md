# Acute and subacute low back pain
Source: ACP Clinical Practice Guideline: Noninvasive Treatments for Acute, Subacute, and Chronic Low Back Pain (2017); educational summary.

## Treatment
Most acute back pain improves within weeks. First choose nonpharmacologic treatment: superficial heat, massage, acupuncture or spinal manipulation, and staying active. If medication is needed, NSAIDs or skeletal muscle relaxants are options. Imaging is not needed in the absence of red flags.

## Red flags
New urinary retention or incontinence, saddle anesthesia or progressive leg weakness (possible cauda equina syndrome), fever or intravenous drug use (infection), history of cancer or unexplained weight loss, and significant trauma require urgent evaluation.
