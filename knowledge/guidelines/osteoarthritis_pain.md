# Osteoarthritis and musculoskeletal pain
Source: 2019 ACR/Arthritis Foundation Guideline for the Management of Osteoarthritis of the Hand, Hip, and Knee; educational summary.

## Treatment
Exercise, weight loss for patients with overweight, and self-management education are strongly recommended. Topical NSAIDs are recommended for knee osteoarthritis. Oral NSAIDs are effective; use the lowest effective dose for the shortest time. Intra-articular corticosteroid injections can help knee and hip osteoarthritis.

## Safety of analgesics
- Oral NSAIDs: avoid or use with great caution in chronic kidney disease, heart failure, uncontrolled hypertension, history of GI bleeding, and with anticoagulants or SSRIs; consider a proton pump inhibitor for GI protection.
- Acetaminophen: no more than 3-4 g per day, lower with liver disease or regular alcohol use; benefit in osteoarthritis is small.
- Tramadol and other opioids are generally not recommended for osteoarthritis.
