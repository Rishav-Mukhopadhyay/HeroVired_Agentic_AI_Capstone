# Type 2 diabetes
Source: American Diabetes Association Standards of Care in Diabetes; educational summary.

## Diagnosis and targets
Diagnosis: HbA1c 6.5% or higher, fasting glucose 126 mg/dL or higher, or 2-hour glucose 200 mg/dL or higher on oral glucose tolerance testing. An HbA1c target below 7% is appropriate for many non-pregnant adults; targets are individualised (less stringent with hypoglycemia risk or limited life expectancy).

## Treatment
Lifestyle and weight management are foundational. Metformin is a common first-line drug. For patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure or chronic kidney disease, an SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven benefit is recommended independent of HbA1c.

## Safety
- Metformin is contraindicated with eGFR below 30; starting it is not recommended with eGFR 30-45. It can cause gastrointestinal upset and vitamin B12 deficiency.
- Sulfonylureas and insulin can cause hypoglycemia.
- SGLT2 inhibitors: genital infections, volume depletion, rare ketoacidosis.

## Monitoring
HbA1c every 3 to 6 months, annual kidney function and urine albumin, eye and foot examinations.
