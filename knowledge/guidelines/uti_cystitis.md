# Acute uncomplicated cystitis (bladder infection) in women
Source: IDSA/ESCMID International Clinical Practice Guidelines for acute uncomplicated cystitis and pyelonephritis in women (2010 update); educational summary.

## Diagnosis
Dysuria, urinary frequency and urgency, suprapubic pain, without fever, flank pain or costovertebral angle tenderness. A urine dipstick positive for leukocyte esterase and/or nitrites supports the diagnosis. Urine culture is recommended for recurrent infection, treatment failure, atypical symptoms, pregnancy, or suspected pyelonephritis.

## First-line treatment
- Nitrofurantoin monohydrate/macrocrystals 100 mg orally twice daily for 5 days. Avoid when pyelonephritis is suspected and in significant renal impairment (creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min).
- Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole 160/800 mg (one double-strength tablet) twice daily for 3 days, only if local resistance is below 20% and the patient has no sulfonamide allergy and no recent use.
- Fosfomycin trometamol 3 g as a single oral dose (slightly lower efficacy).

## Alternatives
Beta-lactams such as amoxicillin-clavulanate, cefdinir or cefpodoxime for 3 to 7 days when first-line agents cannot be used; generally less effective. Fluoroquinolones should be reserved for other infections because of adverse effects and resistance.

## Symptom relief
Phenazopyridine 200 mg three times daily for up to 2 days relieves dysuria; it colours urine orange and does not treat infection.

## Red flags
Fever, flank pain, nausea or vomiting suggest pyelonephritis and need different, longer treatment. Men, pregnant patients and patients with urinary catheters have complicated infections.
