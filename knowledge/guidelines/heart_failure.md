# Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF)
Source: 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA Guideline for the Management of Heart Failure; educational summary.

## Guideline-directed medical therapy
Four medication classes reduce mortality and should be started and titrated as tolerated:
- ARNI (sacubitril-valsartan), or an ACE inhibitor or ARB if ARNI is not possible.
- An evidence-based beta-blocker (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate or bisoprolol).
- A mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (spironolactone or eplerenone).
- An SGLT2 inhibitor (dapagliflozin or empagliflozin).
Loop diuretics such as furosemide relieve congestion but do not improve survival.

## Drugs to avoid
NSAIDs, non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (diltiazem, verapamil) in HFrEF, and thiazolidinediones can worsen heart failure.

## Monitoring and red flags
Monitor potassium and kidney function, daily weights, and symptoms. Rapid weight gain, worsening shortness of breath, orthopnea or edema suggest decompensation and need prompt review.
